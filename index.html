01 / THE FOUNDATIONS

Master the basics.

Simple ideas make money easier to understand. Start with the pieces that shape a monthly budget.

↗01

Income

Money you receive from a job, allowance, or other source.

Example · Monthly allowance
▤02

Fixed expenses

Costs that are usually similar each month and need planning.

Example · Course fee
⌁03

Variable expenses

Costs that can change based on your choices and needs.

Example · Snacks or travel
◇04

Needs

Essentials that support health, safety, or education.

Example · Basic transport
✳05

Wants

Optional extras that can make life enjoyable but can wait.

Example · A new game
◎06

Savings

Money set aside for future plans or unexpected costs.

Example · Laptop fund

04 / COMMON MONEY MOMENTS

Learn from the little slips.

Small patterns are easier to change when you can spot them. Open a card for a student scenario and a practical reset.

01Impulse buyingSpending before you pause＋
The moment

A flash sale tempts you to buy something you hadn't planned for.

Why it matters

Repeated quick purchases can crowd out priorities.

Try this

Wait a day, then decide if it still feels worthwhile.

02Ignoring small expensesLittle purchases add up＋
The moment

Snacks and small rides never make it into your plan.

Why it matters

It becomes harder to explain where the budget went.

Try this

Keep a short weekly note of everyday spending.

03Late paymentsForgetting the due date＋
The moment

A school or household bill slips your mind.

Why it matters

Late fees or service interruptions may follow.

Try this

Add reminders to a calendar you already use.

04Unused subscriptionsPaying for what you don't use＋
The moment

A free trial quietly becomes a recurring charge.

Why it matters

Recurring costs are easy to overlook in a monthly plan.

Try this

Review subscriptions and cancellation dates regularly.

05Spending without a planDecisions made on the fly＋
The moment

You spend first and hope the rest works out later.

Why it matters

Essentials and goals can be harder to prioritize.

Try this

Start with a simple list of income and upcoming costs.
